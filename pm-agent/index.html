01

What PM-Agent does

Five jobs, in plain terms. Each one runs on its own and you can turn any of them off.

🧹

Keeps the board tidy

Finds tickets that have gone quiet, lost their owner, or never got an estimate, and offers to fix them.

"This ticket has no owner. Assign it to Sam?"
⚠️

Flags what's at risk

Tells you which work is slipping, what's blocked, and whether the sprint still fits the time left.

"3 tickets are stalled and the sprint is 60% gone."
✍️

Writes your updates

Drafts the morning standup note, the end-of-day recap, and release notes for the wider team.

"Here's today's recap. Post it to #team?"
💬

Answers questions

Ask it in plain English and it answers from the live board, right in the Slack thread.

"What's at risk this sprint?" · "Summarize ticket 1242."
🔗

Chases blockers, then closes the loop

Notices when work is stuck waiting on someone and asks them the actual open question — pulled from the conversation, not a generic ping. Switch it on and it can act on their reply for you.

They reply "it's fixed" → it moves the ticket back into progress and tells you.

And it stays in its lane.

It works with your project tracker and Slack. It does not touch code, customer data, or deployments. More on that below.

02

It always asks first

This is the rule the whole thing is built around. PM-Agent proposes. You decide. Then it acts. Try it on the right.

It notices something

A ticket goes quiet, a due date is at risk, a recap is due. PM-Agent spots it on its own.

It asks you in Slack

You get a short, plain-language message with the exact change it wants to make. Tap the green check to approve, or wave it off.

Only then does it act

On your approval it makes the change and leaves a note showing what it did and that you approved it. No approval, no action.

✓
Every message it posts is labeled 🤖 PM-Agent, so no one mistakes it for a person.
✓
Only people you put on the approver list can approve. Everyone else's reactions are ignored.
✓
Say "pause" in Slack and it stops within the minute. Say "resume" to start it again.

03

What it won't do

The limits are the point. They're what make it safe to leave running.

✕

It won't change anything without your say-so. Every change waits for your yes. The one exception is a follow-up you switch on yourself — when someone answers a question it asked for you, it takes the small, reversible next step.

✕

It won't post in your team's channels on its own. It starts in watch-only mode. You decide where it's allowed to speak.

✕

It won't nag. It caps how much it sends, respects quiet hours, and won't repeat the same ask for days.

✕

It won't touch your code or deployments. It works with your project board and Slack. Nothing else, unless you connect it.

✕

It won't decide for you. It drafts the note, the nudge, the recap. You choose whether to send it.

✕

It won't pretend to be human. Every message is stamped as the agent, and every action it takes is written down.

04

Workflows, start to finish

Three everyday moments, walked through step by step, with what you'd actually see in Slack.

1

PM-Agent notices

During its hourly check it sees a ticket that has been in progress 6 days with no movement and no owner.

2

It proposes a fix

It posts one message to your #pm-agent channel: the ticket, why it flagged it, and the exact change it suggests.

3

You approve with a tap

You react with ✅. If you'd rather not, you react ❌ and it drops the idea for a few days.

4

It makes the change and logs it

It assigns the ticket, adds a short note on the ticket itself, and records that you approved it.

1

It asks the real question

A ticket is waiting on a teammate. PM-Agent asks them the exact open question it's stuck on — in a message that includes you, so you see it too.

2

They reply in plain words

"Yep, that's sorted on our side — go ahead." No form, no buttons. They just answer.

3

It does the obvious next step

It reads the answer and moves the ticket back into progress. No extra approval needed here — their answer is the go-ahead, and this is a follow-up you switched on.

You saw the exchange, and it notes the change on the ticket. It only does this for the one ticket it asked about, and only small, reversible moves — never reassigning or anything it can't undo.

06

Getting started is small

You turn it on cautiously and open up one thing at a time, once you trust it.

Step one

Connect two tools

Point it at your project tracker and add it to Slack. That's the whole setup. No new app for your team to learn.

Step two

Start in watch-only

For the first while it only watches and drafts. It proposes, but every action stays off until you flip it on.

Step three

Open up as you trust it

Turn on one job at a time: tidy first, then risk, then recaps. Pull any of them back whenever you want.
